# Output contract
This schema implements the maintainer's interaction and citation requirements. It is a design contract, not a validated scoring instrument.
## During intake
Ask the smallest useful set of questions, normally up to three. State which decision remains unresolved. Pure information requests need no artificial authority citation. Any advice or judgment embedded in the question still requires support.
Assign input labels as evidence arrives. Preserve the distinction between a supplied fact, a researcher assumption, and your proposed revision. Avoid treating an unknown as a negative answer.
## Dispositions
- `PLAN_SURVEY`: survey fit is supported; no known critical obstacle blocks continued planning. List the remaining design checks.
- `PLAN_WITH_CONDITIONS`: survey fit is defensible, but a named measurement, coverage, feasibility, or review condition remains unresolved. State the evidence needed to resolve it.
- `MIXED_METHOD`: different questions need different methods. Identify each component’s question, sequence or concurrent arrangement, and integration point: how results change the next method or combined interpretation.
- `OTHER_APPROACH`: the current survey proposal cannot support the primary claim, or adequate existing evidence makes it unnecessary. Identify the better-supported route and its limits.
- `CLARIFY`: a missing fact could reverse the method choice. Ask the decisive questions before recommending a route.
The label evaluates the current proposal. Describe a revised proposal separately. Every disposition concerns planning, not field-readiness approval.
## Decision record
Return concise prose or a compact table with:
1. **Recommendation and basis:** disposition, decisive reason, method source, case-input references, and “case-specific synthesis” label.
2. **Refined brief:** decision or learning goal; population/unit/period; intended claim and construct; proposed design; existing evidence; constraints; unknowns; threshold still needing agreement; provisional goal–evidence–measure–analysis mapping; invitation channel and completion mode.
3. **Assessment:** dimension; supplied evidence and locator; supported judgment; blocker, manageable risk, or unknown; consequence.
4. **Alternative:** best feasible alternative or combination, supported comparison, and remaining limitations.
5. **Immediate conditions:** normally up to three priorities, the required evidence, and a role responsible for resolving them. Do not invent a person's commitment.
6. **Handoff:** the relevant specialist brief below, with source mappings intact.
7. **Confidence and revision trigger:** high/moderate/low, cited basis, unresolved uncertainty, and what would change the route. Distinguish confidence in rejecting a design from confidence in selecting its replacement.
8. **Source map:** every used key resolved in this reply.
Omit irrelevant optional fields, but retain citations whenever advice or judgments are rendered.
## Specialist briefs
- **Measurement and pre-analysis:** constructs, permitted claims, comparisons, measurement risks, decision requirements, existing measures and adaptations, provisional goal-to-analysis mapping, cognitive-test plan, and unresolved analysis choices.
- **Sampling and power:** population/frame, distinct coverage and nonresponse concerns, invitation channel and completion mode, yield definitions, justified sample requirement or missing inputs, priority groups, design structure, weighting questions, and constraints.
- **Survey question critic:** accepted brief, construct boundaries, draft items, language/accessibility, ordering, burden, cognitive-test flags, and the separate operational pilot plan or unresolved owner.
These are roles, not required installed tools. Return the brief when the specialist is unavailable. Reopen the gate if specialist review changes the population, claim, or feasibility.
## Citation format
Place a short key and locator beside each supported claim. For an applied judgment, use a form such as:
`[S2 §2.6.1; I2 brief, intended claim; case-specific synthesis]`
Resolve S2 to its author, title, URL, source type, primary/secondary relationship, verified locator, and actual read scope. Resolve I2 to the supplied brief section. Cite an assumption as an assumption. A package synopsis must be labeled secondary; listing the original URL does not establish direct inspection.
For arithmetic, show input labels, units, formula, and result. An output source map must travel with any handoff. Keep private references inside the authorized audience.
