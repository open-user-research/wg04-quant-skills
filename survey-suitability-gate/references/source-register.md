# Source register
Packaging review date: 25 October 2026. The listed passages were inspected during preparation. This register contains brief secondary summaries, not reproduced source texts. Later agents must describe their own read scope honestly. A registered URL is not evidence of direct inspection in a new run.
Source type describes provenance, not quality. Practitioner articles are primary evidence of their authors' recommendations and generally secondary explanations of the underlying methods. Textbook chapters are methodological references, not original validation studies. Private project files and the private working-group document are not runtime dependencies.

## S6 Recruitment feasibility
Carl J. Pearson, “The Survey Viability Score: My first question in survey planning,” 6 January 2025. Practitioner framework; article inspected.
URL: https://carljpearson.com/the-survey-viability-score-my-first-question-in-survey-planning/
Locators: “A simple formula for a Survey Viability (SV) score”; “Conclusions.”
Supports comparing achievable response yield with the required sample and checking groups separately. R5 makes conservative corrections: reserves reduce capacity, repeated answers do not become independent people, and expected yield is no guarantee. Source simulations are not validation of this skill.
## S7 Power
Danielle Navarro, *Learning Statistics with R*, Chapter 11, “Hypothesis testing.” Author-hosted textbook; §11.8 inspected.
URL: https://learningstatisticswithr.com/11-hypothesistesting.html
Locator: §11.8 Effect size, sample size and power.
Supports power as 1 minus the Type II error rate and its dependence on effect and sample size. This chapter is not a full design-specific power-calculation manual.

## S10 Weighting boundaries
Carl J. Pearson, “Why you should use survey weights in UX research,” 2 April 2025. Practitioner guidance for in-house survey work; relevant sections inspected.
URL: https://carljpearson.com/why-you-should-use-survey-weights-in-ux-research/
Locators: “Population weights,” including calibration and post-stratification; “Putting weights into practice,” including the variable-selection subsection.
Supports benchmark selection, sparse-cell concerns, and weighting expertise. Keep the sampling frame distinct from the population unless the case supports equivalence. Random selection and random assignment address different questions; neither term substitutes for the other.
