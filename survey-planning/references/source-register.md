# Methodological Source Register (WG04 Survey Planning)

Use this reference register to ground methodological recommendations, explain trade-offs to stakeholders, and cite established survey, psychometric, and UX research literature accurately. Never fabricate citations, page numbers, or empirical validation claims.

---

## 1. Core Survey & Quantitative UX Research Books

- **Sauro, J., & Lewis, J. R. (2024).** *Surveying the User Experience: Design and Analysis of Surveys for UX and Customer Research.* MeasuringU Press. (ISBN `978-1-7333392-1-6`)
  - *Key coverage:* When a survey is the right method (Ch. 1, pp. 11–14); Four Types of Survey Error—coverage, sampling, nonresponse, measurement (Ch. 2, pp. 20–24); Construct definition & existing scale selection (Ch. 6, pp. 64–66); Memory distortion, reconstruction bias, and reference windows (Ch. 9, pp. 91–92); Rating scale architecture & number of points; Pretesting and soft launching (Ch. 18, pp. 296–298).
- **Jarrett, C. (2021).** *Surveys That Work: A Practical Guide for Designing and Running Better Surveys.* Rosenfeld Media. (ISBN `978-1-933820-53-8`)
  - *Key coverage:* Separating research questions from survey questions and challenging question ideas against the decision (Ch. 1); The four steps to answer a question and cognitive interviewing (Ch. 3); Choosing delivery mode (Spotlight G); Operational pilot testing (Ch. 5).
- **Chapman, C., & Rodden, K. (2023/2024).** *Quantitative User Experience Research: Informing Product Decisions by Understanding Users at Scale.* Apress / Springer. (DOI: `10.1007/978-1-4842-9268-6`)
  - *Key coverage:* Core pillars of Quant UXR; HEART and Goals–Signals–Metrics alignment; MaxDiff and survey methods in product organizations.
- **Sauro, J., & Lewis, J. R. (2016).** *Quantifying the User Experience: Practical Statistics for User Research* (2nd ed.). Morgan Kaufmann.
  - *Key coverage:* Confidence intervals for binary proportions (Adjusted-Wald / Wilson) and continuous means; sample size planning for usability and survey studies; standardized UX questionnaires (`SUS`, `PSSUQ`, `CSUQ`, `SEQ`, `SUM`, `SUPR-Q`).
- **Valliant, R., Dever, J. A., & Kreuter, F. (2018).** *Practical Tools for Designing and Weighting Survey Samples* (2nd ed.). Springer. (DOI: `10.1007/978-3-319-93632-1`)
  - *Key coverage:* Stratified sampling, design weights, nonresponse adjustments, post-stratification, raking/calibration, weight trimming, and Kish's Design Effect (`DEFF`).
- **Groves, R. M., Fowler, F. J., Couper, M. P., Lepkowski, J. M., Singer, E., & Tourangeau, R. (2009).** *Survey Methodology* (2nd ed.). Wiley.
  - *Key coverage:* Total Survey Error (TSE) framework separating errors of non-observation (coverage, sampling, nonresponse) from errors of observation (construct validity, measurement error, processing error).
- **Dillman, D. A., Smyth, J. D., & Christian, L. M. (2014 / 2000).** *Internet, Phone, Mail, and Mixed-Mode Surveys: The Tailored Design Method.* Wiley.
  - *Key coverage:* Question writing principles, construct-specific scales over Agree/Disagree items, visual layout, and reducing respondent burden.
- **Navarro, D. (2019).** *Learning Statistics with R: A Tutorial for Psychology Students and Other Beginners.* (`https://learningstatisticswithr.com/`)
  - *Key coverage:* Study design, operationalization, internal/external/construct validity, nonresponse bias (Ch. 2); Hypothesis testing, effect size, sample size, and statistical power (Ch. 11, §11.8).
- **Ladner, S. (2019).** *Mixed Methods: A Short Guide to Applied Mixed Methods Research.* (ISBN `978-1-7342178-0-3`)
  - *Key coverage:* Sequential and concurrent mixed-methods designs, priority routing, and explicit integration points across qualitative and quantitative evidence.
- **Gorvine, B. J., Rosengren, K. S., Stein, L., & Biolsi, K. (2018).** *Research Methods: From Theory to Practice.* Oxford University Press.
  - *Key coverage:* Question wording checklist (8th–9th grade reading level for general US audiences, avoiding double-barreled and loaded questions, scale balance, midpoint selection, and ordering bias).

## 2. Cognitive Response Process, Visual Design & Pretesting Literature

- **Tourangeau, R., Rips, L. J., & Rasinski, K. (2000).** *The Psychology of Survey Response.* Cambridge University Press.
  - *Key coverage:* The 4-stage cognitive response model (**Comprehension → Retrieval → Judgment → Response Mapping**).
- **Tourangeau, R., & Rasinski, K. A. (1988).** Cognitive processes underlying context effects in attitude measurement. *Psychological Bulletin*, 103(3), 299–314.
  - *Key coverage:* How earlier questions prime retrieval and shift judgments on subsequent broad evaluation items.
- **Couper, M. P., Conrad, F. G., & Tourangeau, R. (2007).** Visual context effects in web surveys. *Public Opinion Quarterly*, 71(4), 623–634.
  - *Key coverage:* How visual spacing, scale layout, and visual cues alter web survey responses.
- **Willis, G. B. (2005).** *Cognitive Interviewing: A Tool for Improving Questionnaire Design.* Sage Publications. (And **Willis, G. B., & Artino, A. R., 2013**, *Journal of Graduate Medical Education*.)
  - *Key coverage:* Think-aloud and verbal probing protocols to uncover hidden comprehension, recall, and response-mapping errors.
- **Bergstrom, J. R., et al. (2017).** *Usability Testing for Survey Research.* Morgan Kaufmann.
  - *Key coverage:* Respondent–survey interaction, planning and conducting usability/cognitive tests on surveys, and iterating on question wording and layout.
- **Pearson, C. J. (2023).** *Cognitive Survey Tests: When UX researchers need to do them (Part 1)* & *Faster methods for wider adoption (Part 2).* Figures & Frameworks (`https://carljpearson.com/cognitive-testing-for-surveys-part-1/`).
  - *Key coverage:* Applying the 4-stage CASM model in UX research; when cognitive testing is critical (complex/abstract items, tracking surveys, high-stakes decisions); rapid unmoderated cognitive testing protocol with `4–5` users (`2–3` per segment).

## 3. Psychometrics, UX Scales, Data Quality & Behavioral Validity

- **Rodden, K., Hutchinson, H., & Fu, X. (2010).** Measuring the user experience on a large scale: User-centered metrics for web applications. *Proceedings of CHI 2010*, 2395–2398.
  - *Key coverage:* The **HEART** framework (Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success) and **Goals–Signals–Metrics (GSM)** process.
- **Brooke, J. (1996).** SUS: A "quick and dirty" usability scale. In *Usability Evaluation in Industry* (pp. 189–194).
- **Lewis, J. R. (1995).** IBM Computer Usability Satisfaction Questionnaires: Psychometric evaluation and instructions for use. *International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction*, 7(1), 57–78. (`PSSUQ`, `CSUQ`, `ASQ`.)
- **Sauro, J., & Lewis, J. R. (2009).** Correlations among prototypical usability metrics: Evidence for the construct of usability. *Proceedings of CHI 2009*, 1609–1618.
  - *Key coverage:* Post-task satisfaction (`SEQ`) correlates more strongly with task completion (`r = .51`), time (`r = -.47`), and errors (`r = -.44`) than global post-test questionnaires (`r = .16–.25`).
- **Hornbæk, K., & Law, E. L.-C. (2007).** Meta-analysis of correlations among usability measures. *Proceedings of CHI 2007*, 617–626. (And **Hertzum, M., 2026**, *International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction*.)
  - *Key coverage:* Subjective perceived usability and objective task performance measure distinct facets of UX (`r ~ .16–.20` person-level); one cannot substitute for the other.
- **Mittal, V., Han, K., Frennea, C., et al. (2023).** Customer satisfaction, loyalty behaviors, and firm financial performance: What 40 years of research tells us. *Marketing Letters*, 34, 171–187.
  - *Key coverage:* Satisfaction correlates `.60` with stated retention intention but `.21` with actual observed retention behavior.
- **de Haan, E., Verhoef, P. C., & Wiesel, T. (2015).** The predictive ability of different customer feedback metrics for retention. *International Journal of Research in Marketing*, 32(2), 195–206. (Also **Morgan & Rego, 2006**; **Keiningham et al., 2007**.)
  - *Key coverage:* Top-2-Box `CSAT` performs as well as or better than `NPS` for predicting customer retention; `NPS` holds no unique predictive advantage.
- **Wu, J., & Du, H. (2012).** Toward a better understanding of behavioral intention and system usage constructs. *European Journal of Information Systems*, 21(6), 680–698. (And **Venkatesh, Speier, & Morris, 2002**.)
  - *Key coverage:* Behavioral intention correlates `r ~ .35` (~12% shared variance) with future logged usage; prior behavior and habit dominate attitudes after initial adoption.
- **Meade, A. W., & Craig, S. B. (2012).** Identifying careless responses in survey data. *Psychological Methods*, 17(3), 437–455.
  - *Key coverage:* Screening for inattentive/careless respondents using response time, longstring (straightlining), bogus/instructed items, psychometric consistency, and Mahalanobis distance.
- **Spector, P. E., Van Katwyk, P. T., Brannick, M. T., & Chen, P. Y. (1997).** When two factors don't reflect two constructs: How item characteristics can produce artifactual factors. *Journal of Management*, 23(5), 659–677.
  - *Key coverage:* Why combining positively and negatively worded items produces method artifacts.
- **Edwards, J. R. (2001).** Ten difference score myths. *Organizational Research Methods*, 4(3), 265–287.
  - *Key coverage:* Reliability and validity traps of subtracting two survey ratings (e.g., expectation minus perception gap scores).

## 4. Applied Quantitative UXR Frameworks (Pearson, Bakhshi & WG04)

- **Bakhshi, S. (2026).** *Survey research in practice.* Quant Research Toolbox Substack (`https://saeidehbakhshi.substack.com/p/survey-research-in-practice`).
  - *Key coverage:* Suitability checks ("should this even be a survey?"), distinguishing insight surveys from measurement/tracking surveys, causal surveys as experiments, and validating surveys as proxies/leading indicators.
- **Pearson, C. J. (2026).** *Qual vs. Quant is more than Why vs. What.* Figures & Frameworks (`https://carljpearson.com/qual-vs-quant-is-more-than-why-vs-what/`).
  - *Key coverage:* Routing research methods by whether elements/categories must be identified (qualitative) vs. counted/modeled (quantitative) rather than superficial "why" vs. "what" labels.
- **Pearson, C. J. (2025).** *The Survey Viability Score: My first question in survey planning* (`https://carljpearson.com/the-survey-viability-score-my-first-question-in-survey-planning/`) & *Sensitivity Penalty (`1 / sqrt(SV)`).*
  - *Key coverage:* Calculating `SV = n_actual / n_planned`, subgroup yield auditing, and translating sample shortfalls into the MDE sensitivity multiplier (`1 / sqrt(SV)`).
- **Pearson, C. J. (2025).** *Minimum Viable Rigor.* Figures & Frameworks (`https://carljpearson.com/minimum-viable-rigor/`).
  - *Key coverage:* Calibrating methodological rigor and precision to the reversibility and risk of the product decision while keeping claim limits honest.
- **Pearson, C. J. (2026).** *Deriving Top-2-Box from Ordinal Models: A Reference for UX Research* (and *Top 2 Box the Right Way*).
  - *Key coverage:* Fitting cumulative link ordinal models (`ordinal::clm`, `VGAM::vglm`, `brms::brm`) to full Likert distributions and deriving Top-2-Box (`P(Y >= K-1)`) post-estimation to preserve statistical power while reporting stakeholder-friendly percentages. (Supported by **Bürkner & Vuorre, 2019**; **Whitehead, 1993**; **Snapinn & Jiang, 2007**.)
- **Pearson, C. J. (2024/2025).** *Why you should use survey weights in UX research (in R).*
  - *Key coverage:* Design weights, nonresponse weights, and post-stratification/raking in R (`survey` and `srvyr` packages) using log-derived behavioral/tenure/country benchmarks.
- **Pearson, C. J. (2026).** *Most UX surveys lag behavior: What it takes to use them as leading indicators.*
  - *Key coverage:* Evidence grades (`A`–`D`) for UX and CX survey constructs against production telemetry and observed task performance.
- **Trochim, W. M. K.** *Research Methods Knowledge Base* (`https://conjointly.com/kb/`).
  - *Key coverage:* Internal/external/construct validity threats and limits of nonprobability/convenience sampling.
